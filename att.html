AT&T Internet FAQs

AT&T offers two main types of residential internet: AT&T Fiber, which uses a fiber-optic line to the home, and AT&T Internet Air, a 5G fixed wireless service offered in some areas where fiber is not available. Which one you can get depends on your service address, per AT&T’s currently published terms.

AT&T Fiber is available in select markets only, and AT&T Internet Air coverage also varies by location. The only way to confirm is to check your exact service address. You can enter your ZIP code on our site or call us, and we will review the options AT&T lists for your address.

Reliability depends on the type of connection at your address. Fiber connections are generally considered more consistent than wireless or older copper-based connections. Wireless services like Internet Air can vary with network congestion, signal strength, and location. Actual performance is determined by AT&T and is not guaranteed by High Speed Fiber Internet.

It depends on how many people and devices use the connection. Lower speed tiers may suit browsing, email, and HD streaming on a few devices. Higher tiers may suit 4K streaming, gaming, remote work, and busy households. Available tiers and pricing vary by service address. Our specialists can walk you through the options AT&T currently lists for your location.

According to AT&T’s published plan information, AT&T Fiber plans are generally offered without annual contracts and with unlimited data. Terms can change and may differ by plan, so please review AT&T’s current terms and the FCC Broadband Facts label before you enroll.

AT&T has offered an income-based program called Access from AT&T for eligible households, for example those in certain government assistance programs. Eligibility, speeds, and pricing are set by AT&T and change over time. Please check AT&T’s official Access program page for current details.

High Speed Fiber Internet does not provide technical support. For slow speeds, outages, equipment, or billing questions, contact AT&T directly. AT&T’s support pages list common steps such as restarting your gateway and checking for local outages.
